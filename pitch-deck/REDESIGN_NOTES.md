# AcquaHT — YC Demo Day Redesign

A complete rebuild of the AcquaHT investor deck around one principle:
**one slide = one idea = one message.** The original was 17 dense, text-heavy
slides. This is 16 core story slides (plus a 5-slide appendix) that a smart
5-year-old could follow, told in the YC arc:

**Problem → Insight → Product → Proof → Market → Moat → Vision → Ask.**

The design stays deliberately "serious deep-tech," not childish: a dark,
premium look where the **color itself carries the thesis** —
**cyan = water / cold (the problem), amber = the free waste heat that fixes it,
coral = cost / pain.** The whole company is "a cold-water problem solved with
hot waste heat," so cyan-vs-amber literally embodies the idea.

> **Nothing was invented.** Every number, customer, grant, partner and claim
> comes from the original deck. Every honesty label the founder used
> (MEASURED / CALCULATED / PROJECTED / ASSUMED / SIGNED) is preserved — moved
> to small, unobtrusive tags so the story stays clean without overstating.

---

## The 5-year-old version of the whole deck

> Air has water in it. We pull it out. But when the air is dry, pulling it out
> costs too much — that's why every machine like ours only works in humid air.
> We found free heat *inside our own machine* to fix that. A sponge grabs the
> water; our waste heat wrings the sponge dry. Same power, more water.
> Customers already pay us — they buy the dry air, and the water comes free.
> We've made real water in the field and signed our first customer. We're the
> only ones who even measure dry air. We're raising **$250K** to prove the
> sponge works.

## Slide-by-slide: headline · supporting text · visual

### 1 — Big Idea
- **Headline:** "Water from air. Even in **dry** air."
- **Support:** *We pull drinking water out of thin air — and keep working where every other machine gives up: dry, inland heat.* + `Hyderabad, India · Founded November 2023`
- **Visual:** Droplet-forming-from-air motif (concentric circles + converging dots). **→ swap in a real product/pilot hero photo.**

### 2 — The Problem
- **Headline:** "In dry air, the same machine makes **75% less water**."
- **Support:** *Same machine. Same power bill. A quarter of the water.*
- **Visual:** Column chart — output **221.7 → 120.2 → 56.1 LPD** across 80/60/30% humidity (bars fade cyan → amber → coral). Side card: cost per litre **₹2.35 → ₹4.32 → ₹9.30**, with "at 30%, more than bottled water."
- **Honesty tag (top-right):** `Calculated · psychrometric`.

### 3 — Why it's hard
- **Headline:** "In dry air, most of the power cools air we just throw away."
- **Support:** *True of every refrigeration machine on the market. That's why every spec sheet — ours once included — is rated at 80% humidity.*
- **Visual:** 100% stacked column — "condenses water" vs "cools air we throw away" across 80/60/30%, plus a giant **75%** callout.

### 4 — The Insight
- **Headline:** "The fix was already inside the machine."
- **Support:** *A desiccant — think of a sponge — grabs water at room temperature. Heat dries it out again. And the heat is already there: our own compressor throws it away.*
- **Visual:** Waste-heat flow: discharge gas **~80°C** → cooled to **45°C** → **1.29 kW** usable free heat. Bottom band: "We're not first — the gap is cost, not physics" (MOF/Berkeley context).

### 5 — The Solution
- **Headline:** "A sponge grabs the water. Our own waste heat wrings it out."
- **Support:** big before/after **56 → 93 LPD** at 30% humidity; *+66% more water — no extra electricity.* Right column: one-sentence "what it is" + "why it matters."
- **Honesty tag (top-right):** `Projected · not yet built`.

### 6 — How it works
- **Headline:** "Three steps."
- **Visual:** 3 numbered cards → **(1)** sponge grabs it · **(2)** waste heat lets it go · **(3)** the fridge makes water. Footnote: everyone else does step 3 only.

### 7 — Proof / Traction  *(strongest slide)*
- **Headline:** "Real machines. Real water. First customer signed."
- **Visual:** three big stat blocks — **210 L** produced (2 pilots, WHO/BIS) · **₹25.9L** raised non-dilutive · **₹75,000** first customer. Bottom band, in amber: *the honest gap — no sorbent has run yet; that's what this round funds.*

### 8 — Customer
- **Headline:** "We sell dehumidification. The water comes free."
- **Visual:** flow — *they already pay for drying* → *with us they get dry air **+ free water*** → *first buyers: dry-climate factories, food processing & cold storage.*

### 9 — Economics
- **Headline:** "Build for ₹1.78L. Sell for ₹3.75L."
- **Visual:** three cards — cost **₹1,78,000** (measured) · price **₹3,75,000** (assumed) · profit **₹1,97,000 / 52.5%**. Plus **₹2,500/mo** recurring and a **4-month** customer-payback callout.

### 10 — Market
- **Headline:** "75 sites we can name and reach — today."
- **Support:** *Not a top-down guess. A bottom-up count across three beachheads.*
- **Visual:** **₹2.81 Cr** hardware + **₹22.5L/yr** service; three numbered beachheads (food/cold storage · coastal · Bala Vikasa villages). Horizon line: once dry-air is proven, every inland site opens up.

### 11 — Competition
- **Headline:** "We're the only ones who even measure dry air."
- **Visual:** two-column contrast — *everyone else: rated only at 80%* vs *AcquaHT: publishes 30% and beats Maithri 3.41 vs 3.13 L/kWh.* Bottom band: **the real competitor is the water tanker.**

### 12 — Moat
- **Headline:** "The hard part isn't the physics. It's doing it at Indian cost."
- **Visual:** four pillars — cheap sponge (IISER partnership) · free heat · real ₹1.78L cost this early (RYLT) · first to measure. Honest note: no patents yet.

### 13 — Plan
- **Headline:** "1 machine today. 250 a year by FY29."
- **Visual:** column chart of units (**1 → 45 → 120 → 250**) + revenue list (₹0.75L → ₹9.38 Cr), breakeven ~90 units, and a one-line roadmap strip. Tag: `FY27–FY29 projected`.

### 14 — Team
- **Headline:** "The founder ran innovation policy for the exact district these machines are built for."
- **Visual:** founder card (Swamynathan Jerra, 100% ownership; mechanical engineer, Siddipet; beVisioneers Fellow 2025; former District Innovation Coordinator) + advisors (Prof. Soumyajit Roy / IISER, Vijender Mogili / Bala Vikasa, RYLT). **→ swap "SJ" circle for a founder photo.**

### 15 — The Ask
- **Headline:** "We're raising **$250,000**."
- **Support:** *Non-dilutive. 24 months of runway to a proven dry-air machine.*
- **Visual:** four use-of-funds cards — **$100k** sorbent · **$50k** waste-heat loop · **$62.5k** manufacturing & certification · **$37.5k** deployment. Bottom band: what unlocks the next raise.

### 16 — Vision (close)
- **Headline:** "The whole industry hides from dry air. **We're building the fix.**"
- **Support:** *If a compressor's own waste heat fixes dry-air economics for us, it fixes them for every refrigeration machine in the world. It starts with 75 machines in Telangana. It doesn't end there.* **→ add founder contact/email.**

### Appendix (A1–A5)
- **A1 — Evidence table:** every number sorted into MEASURED / CALCULATED / PROJECTED / ASSUMED. (Your best diligence slide.)
- **A2 — Full economics & payback:** per-unit economics + the full customer-payback matrix (bottled 4mo / jar 25mo / tanker 4yr / dehumidifier free).
- **A3 — Competition specs:** the Maithri vs AcquaHT table + imported-player note.
- **A4 — Go-to-market:** who/why/how/expand, plus the un-tracked-metrics flag.
- **A5 — Open questions:** the risks you owe investors, stated up front.

## Items flagged, not invented (per your critical rules)

These carry explicit tags in the deck instead of being smoothed over:
- **The desiccant / "sponge" stage has never run in the machine.** Every dry-air
  number (93 LPD, +66%, 1.43 L/kWh) is a **design target** — labelled *projected*
  and called out as "the honest gap" on the proof slide and in A5.
- **₹3,75,000 selling price is assumed**, not yet confirmed with a customer —
  labelled everywhere it drives margin/revenue math.
- **Total dry-climate market size is unknown** — the deck uses the 75 *named*
  sites and explicitly avoids a fabricated TAM.
- **GTM metrics (decision-maker, sales cycle, CAC) aren't tracked yet** — flagged in A4.
- **Certification body/date is open** — flagged in A5.
- **₹75,000 first customer** is preserved as the original labelled it (*signed*);
  A5 keeps the nuance.
